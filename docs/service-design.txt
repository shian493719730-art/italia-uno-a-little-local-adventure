ITALIA UNO — A LITTLE TURN BEHIND THE COUNTER
Original mini-game design and reference record · 7 November 2026

Purpose
The player clicks the fictional owner guide to try a short restaurant-management
practice run. It adds an active service loop to the café exploration rather than
reusing the Cambridge Pledge resource-allocation game. Leaving the practice run,
or finishing all three days, invites the player to visit the real Italia Uno:
“You’ve tried the little game. Why not drop by Italia Uno and see the real café in
action?” There is no claim that this is the actual owner’s voice or that the
virtual results measure the real café’s performance.

Playable loop
1. Before opening, review every order for the day. Buy stock in economical packs.
2. Optionally install ONE improvement for the whole three-day run.
3. Two customer tickets queue at once. Choosing a ticket selects its dish.
4. PREPARE consumes one abstract pantry unit; SERVE pays the ticket’s token price.
5. Cooking, serving, cleaning, emergency restocking and discarding advance waiting
   customers’ patience by one action. Reading, selecting and opening details do
   not advance it. There is no timer, automatic countdown or required quick reflex.
6. Wipe the station after two servings. Keep stock and cash in balance. Every
   day can be closed early, so running out of stock never traps the player.
7. Each day closes with an itemised receipt. Confirm to advance to the next day.
8. After day three, see totals, replay a fresh run or find the real café.

Visible rules and values — all invented for this game
Start: 18 tokens, 2 panino kits, 2 coffee portions.
Day one: 4 guests; day two: 5; day three: 6. Total: 15.
Target: serve at least 11 / 15 guests and finish with at least 35 tokens.
Panino kit: one abstract unit covers bread and featured fillings for any of the
three game panini. This is not an ingredient, allergen or nutritional disclosure.
Prep stock: 3 panino kits for 6 tokens; 4 coffee portions for 4 tokens.
Emergency stock while trading: 1 panino kit for 3; 1 coffee portion for 2; each
uses one action. Stock of each type is capped at 12.
Each espresso pays 3 tokens; each panino pays 6. Serving with at least 4 patience
remaining gives 1 tip token. Prices are game tokens, not pounds or real prices.
Closing cost: 2 tokens daily, announced before opening and reserved by purchase
checks. It is charged once on closing, explicitly listed in the receipt.
Initial patience: 10 on day one, 8 on day two, 7 on day three.
One 10-token upgrade, optional and permanent within the practice run:
- Prep counter: panino preparation costs no patience; serving still costs one.
- Grinder: each served espresso pays one extra token.
- Waiting corner: each new guest arrives with three more patience.
Stock, till and improvement carry between days; a reset clears the entire run.
Neither absence, poverty, wages nor the original game’s flower mechanics apply.

Original implementation and reuse boundary
lib/service-state.mjs is an immutable deterministic reducer with guarded phase
transitions, unique finite customer IDs and explicit action costs. It has no
backend, third-party agent, API call, randomness or fabricated reviews.
app/service-shift.tsx is bilingual English / Chinese. Customer figures, food,
espresso machine, counter, upgrades and shopfront are small original CSS pixel
sprites. No external game code, sprite, sound or photo was copied for this
mini-game. All interactions are keyboard buttons; explanation panels are ordinary
text; confirmation prompts trap focus and restore it on closing. Visual feedback
lists cash and stock before/after. Patience is numeric as well as a visible bar.

Validation
Command: node tests/service.test.mjs
55 meaningful state assertions pass, covering opening/stock/upgrade costs,
immutability, stock capacity, reserved closing costs, preparation, wrong-dish and
out-of-stock feedback, emergency restocking, cleaning, repeated-action guards,
finite queue loss, early closing, one-time settlement, day transitions, all three
upgrade effects, full three-day success, finished-state guards and reset.
Reachable demonstration strategy: buy only the stock needed before opening,
serve the oldest ticket and wipe after two servings. It serves all 15 guests and
finishes with 73 tokens without an upgrade. Poor stocking or wasted service
actions can still lose guests. No real-time timer is used.
TSX transpilation check: TypeScript transpileModule, React JSX, ES2022 — passed.
Full browser integration and build are performed in the parent project workflow.

Integration
import ServiceShift from './service-shift';
Render <ServiceShift lang={lang} onExit={closePanel} onSound={soundEffect} /> in a
wide, scrollable panel. onSound receives select, serve, error or win. Appending
docs/service-css.txt to app/globals.css styles the mini-game. All selectors use
shift-* to avoid altering the root café scene, menu or exploration UI.
Mounting a new instance starts a fresh practice run; reopening does not overwrite
or reset the exploration visit stamps / private notebook.
